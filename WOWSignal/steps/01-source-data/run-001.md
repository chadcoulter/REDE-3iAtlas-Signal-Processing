## 2. Establish orientation and positions

- Source data rows: **82**, in original top-to-bottom order.
- Intensity columns: **50**, labelled **01–50** from left to right.
- Loaded array: **82 rows × 50 columns**, addressed as `grid[row_0based][channel_0based]`.
- The narrative's 50 × 82 frequency/time dimensions therefore describe the same data with axes listed in the opposite order; the stored array is not transposed.
- Channel number equals array column index plus one.
- CSV line equals array row index plus two, including its header; TXT line equals row index plus one.
- Printed right ascension, declination, second LO frequency, galactic coordinates, and Eastern Standard Time are retained per row.
- No absolute per-channel sky frequencies or corrected celestial coordinates were calculated.

The [raw grid](run-001/raw_grid.json), [row metadata](run-001/row_metadata.csv), and [cell records](run-001/cells.csv) explicitly preserve this ordering.

## 4. Preserve numeric information

Applied the [observatory's intensity coding](https://bigear.org/6equj5.htm):

- Space: printed bin 0.
- Characters 1–9: corresponding integer bin.
- Letters A–Z: printed bins 10–35.

Every source character is preserved alongside its decoded bin. Bin labels describe truncated, baseline-subtracted, rms-scaled intensity; they do not recover unrecorded precision. Nominal lower-inclusive and upper-exclusive bounds are retained per cell, under the ordinary no-rollover interpretation. The original observatory assessment reports no rollover for the Wow! sequence.

Verified that decoding and re-encoding all **4,100 cells** restores their original characters. A blank remains a blank in the raw grid; it is explicitly identified when represented as numeric bin zero.

The full [numeric grid](run-001/numeric_grid.json) is retained, not just the six famous samples.
