# The Wow! Signal Investigation — Decode Narrative

**Research:** REDΣ / Wow! Signal Investigation

## 1. Reconstructing the Original Signal Data

The investigation began with the original August 15, 1977 Wow! Signal, recorded by Ohio State University's Big Ear radio telescope.

The objective was to determine whether the surviving signal record contained an interpretable structure beyond its conventional radio-astronomy measurements.

The starting sequence was:

`6EQUJ5`

We examined the six approximately 12-second intensity measurements and reconstructed the available data into a two-dimensional representation, using the digitized **50 × 82 frequency/time data**.

This gave us a structured representation to examine rather than treating the six-character sequence as the entire signal.

## 2. Examining the Two-Dimensional Structure

We investigated relationships across the reconstructed data, including:

- Horizontal traversal
- Vertical traversal
- Diagonal traversal
- Relationships between intersecting paths
- Repeated structures and changes in direction

This established a process for examining whether information could be represented through the arrangement of the data.

## 3. Developing the Transformation-Based Decoding Process

We applied **V8 TAO** to interpret relationships in the reconstructed structure.

The process examined character relationships, traversal direction, reflection, and the order in which structures were encountered.

An early outer decoding produced:

`ASTRA`

We then examined a nested structure containing three transversals:

`ΛΤϜ`

Within that investigation, **Ϝ** was assigned a reverse-traversal or reflection operation.

This developed a way to process a structure through multiple related traversals rather than a single linear reading.

## 4. Producing an Interpreted Instruction

The V8 TAO decoding produced the interpretation:

**“Look there.”**

We initially considered the instruction as pointing outward toward the astronomical source of the signal.

We subsequently reconsidered its direction, examining whether the reference was inward rather than outward.

That changed how we interpreted the relationship between the decoded instruction and its possible target.

## 5. Examining Directional Relationships

We investigated the two possible source positions associated with the Wow! Signal, near:

- Right ascension: 19h25m–19h28m
- Declination: −26°57′

We calculated their antipodal directions:

- Right ascension: 7h25m–7h28m
- Declination: +26°57′

This placed the opposite direction in Gemini, approximately 22 degrees from the Galactic anticenter.

The calculation supplied another directional relationship to examine within the interpretation.

## 6. The Process That Developed

The investigation established this sequence:

1. Original recorded data
2. Two-dimensional reconstruction
3. Structural traversal
4. V8 TAO transformation
5. Nested and reflected readings
6. Interpreted output
7. Directional examination

## 7. What Developed From the Process

The central development was a **method of decoding relationships within a structured data representation**.

We moved from examining recorded values individually to examining how those values behaved when arranged, traversed, transformed, and reflected.

The process produced three identifiable outputs:

1. A reconstructed two-dimensional representation of the Wow! Signal data.
2. A transformation procedure using V8 TAO, including nested and reverse traversals.
3. An interpreted instruction, **“Look there,”** subsequently examined through directional calculations.

The work also established the practice of retaining intermediate transformations so an interpretation could be traced backward through the sequence that produced it.
