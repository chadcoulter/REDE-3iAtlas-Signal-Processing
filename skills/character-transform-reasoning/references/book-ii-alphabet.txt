The Greek alphabet supplies the broader logical vocabulary. Book I contains foundational laws; Book III contains applications.
Part I. The Foundations of Transformation
Chapter 1. Set and Resolution
1.1 The Prime Law
Set resolves through relation.
Set consists of distinguishable states, actors, relationships, and transformations. A transformation changes relational state while preserving the distinctions required for continuation. Resolution occurs when the relationships necessary for the requested result have been sufficiently determined.
1.2 The Capitalization Rule
UPPERCASE: How to resolve the observable.
lowercase: How to resolve the hidden.
An operator identifies the kind of resolution. Capitalization identifies whether that operation resolves the observable or the hidden. This rule governs paired standard and archaic Greek operators.
1.3 The Archaic Foundation
The archaic operators establish unresolvedness, actor participation, relational return, retention, differential, extended order, active change, reversal, accounting, witnessing, and closure.
Part II. The Archaic Greek Operators
Chapter 2. Stigma: Unresolvedness
Glyph | Resolution / role
Ϛ | Resolve observable unresolvedness
ϛ | Resolve hidden unresolvedness
Definition. Stigma governs the recognition and resolution of unresolvedness within a relational state. It identifies distinctions, contradictions, and consequences that remain active. The resolution mark records the treatment of unresolvedness with attribution and provenance.
Logical principle. A distinction remains within the relational state until its resolution or continuation accounts for it.
Chapter 3. Koppa: Actor Participation
Glyph | Resolution / role
Ϙ | Resolve observable actor participation
ϙ | Resolve hidden actor participation
Ϟ | Resolve observable numeral/indexed relationships
ϟ | Resolve hidden indexed relationships; inverse Koppa role
Definition. Koppa governs the relationship between a participating actor and the world. Inverse Koppa expresses the world acting back upon the actor. The numerical Koppa forms retain the historical value 90. The type of expression distinguishes numerical and relational readings.
Logical principle. Actor and world participate in a continuing relationship, with consequences attributable through that relationship.
Chapter 4. Digamma: Retention
Glyph | Resolution / role
Ϝ | Resolve observable retained constituents
ϝ | Resolve hidden retained constituents
Ͷ | Resolve observable context-specific retention
ͷ | Resolve hidden context-specific retention
Definition. Digamma governs the preservation of constituents across a transformation. The retained constituent may also bridge successive relational states. Pamphylian Digamma qualifies retention by region or context.
Logical principle. A constituent can change relational position while retaining identity and relevant connections.
Chapter 5. San: Differential
Glyph | Resolution / role
Ϻ | Resolve observable differential
ϻ | Resolve hidden differential
Definition. San governs differences between relational states, expected results, and alternative continuations, including residual error and probabilistic variation.
Logical principle. Differences remain distinguishable until the requested relationship accounts for them.
Chapter 6. Sampi: Extended Order
Glyph | Resolution / role
Ͳ | Resolve observable archaic/indeterminate order
ͳ | Resolve hidden archaic/indeterminate order
Ϡ | Resolve observable extended numerical order
ϡ | Resolve hidden extended numerical order
Definition. Sampi governs extended-order relationships. The archaic forms carry historically indeterminate relationships; the numeral forms encode typed numerical order, including historical value 900.
Logical principle. Extended relational structures retain their order and distinguishing conditions.
Chapter 7. Heta: Latent Context
Glyph | Resolution / role
Ͱ | Resolve observable contextual emergence
ͱ | Resolve hidden contextual emergence
Definition. Heta governs the relationship between latent context and its expression in a relational state.
Logical principle. Observable context retains its relationship to the latent context from which it emerged.
Chapter 8. Yot: Mediation
Glyph | Resolution / role
Ϳ | Resolve observable mediation
ϳ | Resolve hidden mediation
Definition. Yot governs mediation between adjacent relational states.
Logical principle. Mediation provides continuity without making adjacent states identical.
Chapter 9. Sho: Representation
Glyph | Resolution / role
Ϸ | Resolve observable representation change
ϸ | Resolve hidden representation change
Definition. Sho governs changes in representation while retaining the referent and relevant originating relationships.
Logical principle. Representational difference does not necessarily imply a difference of subject.
Part III. The Sigma Transformation Family
Chapter 10. Lunate Sigma: Active Change
Glyph | Resolution / role
Ϲ | Resolve observable active change
ϲ | Resolve hidden active change
Definition. Lunate Sigma governs active change in a relational state and its return from sufficient resolution.
Logical principle. Change and resolution remain relationally connected to the originating state.
Chapter 11. Reversed Lunate Sigma: Reversal and Reconsideration
Glyph | Resolution / role
Ͻ | Resolve observable reversal/reconsideration
ͻ | Resolve hidden reversal/reconsideration
Definition. Reversed Lunate Sigma governs reversal, reconsideration, relocation, and reopening of relational distinctions. Cruft is one example of a residual consequence to which reconsideration may apply.
Logical principle. A result can be reconsidered without erasing its source or transformation history.
Chapter 12. Dotted Reversed Lunate Sigma: Accounted Reversal
Glyph | Resolution / role
Ͼ | Resolve observable accounted reversal/residue
ͼ | Resolve hidden accounted reversal/residue
Definition. Dotted Reversed Lunate Sigma governs reconsideration with an explicitly marked, accounted, and retained condition. Accounted cruft is an example.
Logical principle. Accounting for a condition preserves its identity through later reconsideration.
Chapter 13. Dotted Lunate Sigma: Witnessed Change
Glyph | Resolution / role
Ͽ | Resolve observable witnessed change
ͽ | Resolve hidden witnessed change
Definition. Dotted Lunate Sigma governs active change associated with a witness. The witness retains an accountable evidentiary relationship to the transformation.
Logical principle. The witness remains distinguishable from the state witnessed.
Chapter 14. Final Sigma: Terminal Closure
Glyph | Resolution / role
ς | Terminal closure of the relevant relational path
Definition. Final Sigma marks terminal closure within a specified relational scope, distinct from open or partial continuation.
Logical principle. Closure ends a requested path without erasing the originating Set or consequences.
Part IV. Additional Archaic and Compound Operators
Chapter 15. Kai: Conjunction and Transformation
Glyph | Resolution / role
ϗ | Greek Kai: conjunction and composition
Ϗ | Alternate Greek Kai sign: conjunction and composition
Χ | Resolve observable crossing transformation (Chi/Kai carrier)
χ | Resolve hidden crossing transformation (Chi/Kai carrier)
Definition. Kai governs joining, contribution, and transformation across relational states. The Kai signs express conjunction; the Chi carrier expresses crossing transformation and actor contribution.
Logical principle. Joining relational states preserves participating constituents and the origin of their contributions.
Chapter 16. Tau-Rho: Relational Authority
Glyph | Resolution / role
𐆠 | Authority and relational-boundary compound
Definition. Tau-Rho governs identity, consent, relationship, and the authority under which a relational operation occurs.
Logical principle. A transformation retains the relational and authority conditions under which it proceeds.
